Expense Tracker (KES Books): Privacy Policy

Last updated: 8 November 2026

Expense Tracker (“the app”, in-app brand KES Books) is an on-phone accounting and expense-tracking app for Android, published by Joel Harold Onyango (H0J-MQH Holdings Limited). This policy explains what the app accesses, why, and where that data goes.

Summary: the app has no developer server and no online accounts. Your books live only on your phone, encrypted. The app shows no ads, uses no analytics, and does not sell or share your data. Nothing leaves your device unless you choose one of the two optional features in section 3.

1. Data the app stores and why

The app is a bookkeeping tool. Everything you enter — expenses, invoices, bills, customers, vendors, employees and payroll figures, bank entries, fixed assets, journals, receipt photos, budgets and settings — is stored only on your device, in the app’s private storage, in an encrypted database (the key is generated on your phone and never leaves it). Printed PDF documents are additionally locked with your login password (AES-256): the same password opens them.

User accounts in the app are local accounts on your phone. There is no default account: on first launch you create the administrator account yourself. Passwords are stored only as salted PBKDF2 hashes. An on-device audit log records sign-ins and bookkeeping actions (who did what, when) — it never records passwords or codes.

2. Permissions

Permission Why
INTERNET Runs the app’s own pages locally on your phone (127.0.0.1), and carries the two optional features in section 3. With those features unused, no data is transmitted.
USE_BIOMETRIC Optional app lock with fingerprint/face or your screen lock. Android handles the biometrics; the app never sees them.

Receipt photos are taken with your phone’s camera app (the app itself has no camera permission) and read by on-device text recognition (Google ML Kit, bundled model — works offline; the image is not uploaded).

3. Optional features that send data off the device

Both are off until you turn them on, and both use your own accounts, not ours:

  1. AI insights. You may paste your own Google Gemini or OpenAI API key and ask questions about your books. Only then is the question and the relevant expense data sent, over HTTPS, directly to the provider you chose. The provider’s own privacy policy governs that request. The developer runs no server and receives nothing. Your API key is stored encrypted on the device (Android Keystore) and sent only to that provider.
  2. E-mailed sign-in codes. The administrator may enter their own e-mail account (e.g. a Gmail app password) on the Admin page. After that, each login or sign-up sends a 6-digit code to the user’s e-mail address through the administrator’s own mail account, over TLS. The mail password is stored only in the phone’s encrypted database. If no mail account is entered, nothing is ever e-mailed and sign-in works without codes.

4. Backups

The app keeps rotating encrypted backups in its private storage and lets you export an encrypted backup file to save wherever you choose (Google Drive, e-mail, a computer). Exported backups stay encrypted; you choose where they go, and that destination’s own policy applies.

5. Data deletion

Everything is yours to delete: delete entries in the app (a recycle bin asks for confirmation and allows restore), or delete all data at once via Android Settings → Apps → Expense Tracker → Storage → Clear data, or uninstall the app. Because there is no server, there is nothing for the developer to delete — we never had your data. Data you chose to send to an AI provider is governed by that provider’s deletion process.

6. Children

The app is a business bookkeeping tool intended for adults (18+). It is not directed at children and collects no data from anyone.
